## The clearest reasons to interview David

### He can define a product when the brief is still unstable

David’s strongest work begins before the interface is obvious. He looks for the real bottleneck, tests assumptions with the people closest to it, and changes direction when the evidence disagrees with the original concept.

Filo is the clearest example. It began as EcoFabriq, a biomaterial marketplace with a chatbot built during an MIT × Polimi hackathon. ## Selected work

### Momo — multimodal AI reflection system for behavioral change

**Role:** Lead Product Designer  
**Project period:** March–September 2025  
**Team shown in the case study:** two developers, two designers, and two therapists

Momo explores how a wearable, voice reflection, and AI could help someone notice a difficult moment, talk through what happened, and prepare for a similar situation next time. The product pairs body signals with situational context and a short voice reflection. It avoids claiming that the system can diagnose emotion; the person supplies the meaning.

David’s responsibilities and evidence include:

- End-to-end product design across the Watch, phone, reflection flow, connected insights, and tracking experience.
- Collaboration with engineers, researchers, and therapists.

- A 15-day in-the-wild study with 20 participants.
- Weekly action plans increasing from 3.33 to 6.53.
- Eleven participants asking to keep using the experience.
- Clear separation between what the study validated and what remained a future hypothesis.

Recruiter signal: zero-to-one product thinking, cross-device UX, responsible AI framing, behavior-change research, and the ability to turn a complex system into a coherent product story.

### Filo — agentic commerce for biomaterial adoption in fashion

**Role:** Product design and user research  
**History:** MIT × Polimi hackathon in late 2024, interviews in 2025, redesign in 2026  
**Team shown in the case study:** one engineer, two fashion designers, and one product designer

Filo began as EcoFabriq, a marketplace intended to help designers discover biomaterials. Eleven interviews showed that designers could already discover materials; the adoption risk came later. David reframed the product around the technical designer responsible for proving that a material can be sampled, sewn, tested, manufactured, and scaled in time for a collection.

The redesigned concept:

- Accepts a material or swatch as input.
- Identifies known and missing technical information.
- Proposes an adoption plan, budget, and go/no-go date.
- Coordinates sample requests and comparisons.
- Organizes lab results and factory constraints into a technical file.
- Reports capacity, lead time, price, and gaps at multiple production volumes.
- Preserves human approval for money and messages.

The case study is candid that parts of the current prototype are staged and states what David would test next.

Recruiter signal: research-led reframing, agent design, workflow design, enterprise complexity, and comfort admitting what is simulated versus validated.
